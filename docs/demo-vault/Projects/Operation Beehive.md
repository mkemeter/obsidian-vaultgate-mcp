# Operation Beehive

## Objective

Modernise the Springfield DMV's core transaction system — a mainframe originally installed in 1987 that processes driver's licence issuance, vehicle registration, and exam scheduling. The system is stable, undocumented, and actively defended by the people who use it.

Project codename: **Beehive** — inspired by the hairstyle famously associated with Springfield's most iconic residents. Also appropriate because: highly structured, impossible to get into uninvited, and stings if you disturb it.

## Status: On Hold #on-hold

The project is on hold because [[Springfield DMV]] (contacts: Patty and Selma Bouvier) have declined every proposal submitted to date.

Proposals submitted:
1. Initial discovery questionnaire — returned blank.
2. System assessment request — email response: "No."
3. Phased migration proposal (47 pages, authored by [[Lisa Simpson]]) — email response: "No. The current system has worked since 1987. Please stop sending proposals."
4. Informal conversation request — no response.

[[Homer Simpson]] is technically the project lead. He has described the engagement as "actually really relaxing." He has not sent a proposal since number three. He is not sure this counts as a problem.

## Why It's Stuck

Patty and Selma view the 1987 mainframe not as a risk but as a feature. It does what they need it to do. They trained on it. They understand it. They do not understand, nor wish to understand, what "cloud-native modernisation" means or why they should want it.

[[Lisa Simpson]] has flagged the DMV mainframe as the top item in [[Tech Debt]]. The City contract (see [[City of Springfield]]) nominally includes DMV modernisation in scope. The DMV was not party to that contract.

## Next Steps

Unclear. I have the project on hold pending a strategic decision: escalate through [[City of Springfield]] / [[City of Springfield#Contact]], or accept that the 1987 mainframe will outlast everyone involved.
