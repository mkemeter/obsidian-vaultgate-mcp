# City of Springfield

## Company

The municipal government of Springfield. Budget: perpetually revised. Governance: opaque. Procurement: a formality. The City of Springfield signed with CGHMN after a competitive tender process in which CGHMN was the only bidder who submitted documentation on time. Mayor Quimby described this as "the people's IT contract."

The city operates dozens of legacy systems, most of which interact with each other through undocumented integrations and institutional memory. The permit portal — the subject of [[Project Monorail]] — was last updated when the city's IT director retired. Nobody hired a replacement.

## Contact

**Mayor Joseph "Diamond Joe" Quimby** — elected official, primary signatory on the CGHMN contract, and primary source of scope additions. Kennedy-esque accent. Speaks in campaign cadences regardless of context. "Er, ah…" precedes most statements of substance.

*"As I have always said, er, ah… the people of Springfield deserve the finest IT infrastructure their tax dollars can provide — within the, ah, revised budget."*

*"I am not a crook… except in the strictly legal sense."*

*"Vote Quimby."* — appended to two formal project emails, apparently by habit.

## Engagement

**Project:** [[Project Monorail]]
**Status:** `planning` — health `yellow`

A city permit portal modernisation. [[Lisa Simpson]] is the lead and has already documented four risks that Quimby has not acknowledged. The original scope was a simple permit lookup tool. It has since expanded to include: mobile app, multilingual support, integration with the county assessor's office, a "citizen satisfaction dashboard," and a public-facing mayoral message widget. Budget has not changed.
